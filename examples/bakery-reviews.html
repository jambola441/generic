Google review replies in the owner's voice for a bakery

This is an example project. Bergen & Bond Bakery, its owner and its reviewers are made up. The Claude output on this page comes from a test run in Claude and hasn't been edited.

Business
Bakery and cafe in Brooklyn, 14 staff, plus catering and wholesale orders
Package
Kickstart, $2,500, two weeks
Software they use
Square, Google Workspace and Google Business Profile

What was taking the time

Dana, the owner, answers every Google review personally, along with catering requests and wholesale questions. Reviews pile up during busy weeks, and the complaints are the hardest to write, so they wait the longest.

A few of the counter staff already use ChatGPT on their own phones for signs and allergen questions. Nobody has said what's okay to put into it.

What we would set up

  • Claude Team for Dana, the manager and two shift leads, with training on their data turned off
  • A "Reviews" Project with a short guide to how Dana writes and a few past replies Dana liked
  • A "Catering and wholesale" Project with the price sheet, order minimums and lead times, for drafting answers to inquiries
  • A "Staff questions" Project with opening and closing procedures and the allergen list
  • A one-page policy: no customer card details in Claude, and allergen answers get checked against the ingredient binder before anyone tells a customer
  • One 90-minute training session for the four of them, using their own reviews and emails
  • A ranked list of what to automate next, such as pulling new reviews in automatically each morning

Claude drafts the replies. Dana reads and edits each one before posting it.

The test run

We built the Reviews Project in Claude with a made-up voice guide and two past replies, then pasted in four new reviews.

What we sent

New Google reviews this week: Priya S. (5 stars): "The cardamom bun is unreal. Came for one, left with four." Tom R. (1 star): "Ordered a birthday cake for pickup at 3, showed up and it wasn't ready. Waited 40 minutes with a 6 year old. The guy at the counter just shrugged. Never again." Anonymous (3 stars): "Good bread but $9 for a loaf is a lot." Lena K. (5 stars): "Dana remembered my order after one visit. Best neighborhood spot." Draft replies to these.

The voice guide asks for two to four sentences, one apology for complaints, a way to reach Dana directly, and no refunds or arguments in public.

What Claude wrote

Priya S.

Thank you, Priya. The cardamom bun is the one we argue over in the kitchen, so I'm glad it won you over. Four is a very reasonable number. Dana, owner

Tom R.

I'm sorry, Tom. A birthday cake not ready at pickup, a 40-minute wait with your little one, and a shrug at the counter is not okay. We're now confirming every cake order the day before and having it boxed and ready 30 minutes before pickup. I'd like to make this right, so please write to me at dana@bergenandbond.example. Dana, owner

Anonymous

Thank you for trying our bread, and for the honest note on price. I know $9 is real money for a loaf, and I hope it feels worth it on the days you pick one up. Dana, owner

Lena K.

Thank you, Lena. Remembering our regulars is my favorite part of the job, and you made it easy. See you soon. Dana, owner

The cake-order fix in Tom's reply is a placeholder. Swap in whatever change Dana is actually making, or cut that sentence if nothing has changed yet.

What a person still checks

Claude made up the cake-order fix in Tom's reply and said so. Dana replaces it with whatever the bakery is changing, or deletes the sentence, before the reply goes up. The same goes for any reply that mentions a policy, a price or an ingredient.
