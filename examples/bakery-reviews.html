A bakery’s Google review replies, in the owner’s voice

This is an example. Bergen & Bond Bakery, its owner and its reviewers are made up. The AI output on this page is from a test run in Claude, the AI we use, and hasn’t been edited.

Business
Bakery and cafe in Brooklyn, 14 staff, plus catering and wholesale orders
Package
Kickstart, $2,500, two weeks
Software they use
Square, Google Workspace and Google Business Profile

Where the time went

Dana owns the bakery and answers every Google review herself, along with catering requests and wholesale questions. In busy weeks the reviews pile up. The complaints are the hardest to write, so they wait the longest.

A few of her counter staff already use ChatGPT on their phones for signs and allergen questions. Nobody has told them what’s okay to put into it.

What we’d set up

  • A company AI account for Dana, her manager and two shift leads. It doesn’t learn from their data.
  • A “Reviews” workspace with a short guide to how Dana writes and a few past replies she liked
  • A “Catering and wholesale” workspace with the price sheet, order minimums and lead times, for answering inquiries
  • A “Staff questions” workspace with opening and closing steps and the allergen list
  • A one-page policy. No customer card details go into the AI, and allergen answers get checked against the ingredient binder before anyone tells a customer.
  • One 90-minute training session for the four of them, using their own reviews and emails
  • A ranked list of what to automate next, like pulling in new reviews every morning

The AI drafts the replies. Dana reads and edits each one, then posts it herself.

We tried it

We set up the reviews workspace with a made-up writing guide and two past replies. Then we pasted in four new reviews.

What we gave the AI

New Google reviews this week: Priya S. (5 stars): "The cardamom bun is unreal. Came for one, left with four." Tom R. (1 star): "Ordered a birthday cake for pickup at 3, showed up and it wasn't ready. Waited 40 minutes with a 6 year old. The guy at the counter just shrugged. Never again." Anonymous (3 stars): "Good bread but $9 for a loaf is a lot." Lena K. (5 stars): "Dana remembered my order after one visit. Best neighborhood spot." Draft replies to these.

Dana’s guide says: two to four sentences, say sorry once, give her email for complaints, and never offer refunds or argue in public.

What the AI wrote back

Priya S.

Thank you, Priya. The cardamom bun is the one we argue over in the kitchen, so I'm glad it won you over. Four is a very reasonable number. Dana, owner

Tom R.

I'm sorry, Tom. A birthday cake not ready at pickup, a 40-minute wait with your little one, and a shrug at the counter is not okay. We're now confirming every cake order the day before and having it boxed and ready 30 minutes before pickup. I'd like to make this right, so please write to me at dana@bergenandbond.example. Dana, owner

Anonymous

Thank you for trying our bread, and for the honest note on price. I know $9 is real money for a loaf, and I hope it feels worth it on the days you pick one up. Dana, owner

Lena K.

Thank you, Lena. Remembering our regulars is my favorite part of the job, and you made it easy. See you soon. Dana, owner

The cake-order fix in Tom's reply is a placeholder. Swap in whatever change Dana is actually making, or cut that sentence if nothing has changed yet.

What Dana still does

The AI made up the cake-order fix in Tom’s reply, and said so. Dana swaps in whatever the bakery is changing, or cuts the sentence, before she posts it. She does the same with any reply that mentions a policy, a price or an ingredient.
