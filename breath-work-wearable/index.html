Breath work wearable

A small clip for breathing exercises. Two magnetic discs on a short ribbon hold each other through your shirt, at the neckline or the cuff. The disc against your skin carries a vibration motor that swells and fades with a breathing pattern. Your phone reads your breathing from the movement of your chest and either paces you or follows you.

This is a side project by the person behind generic.tech, not a product and not for sale. The notes, parts and files are here so anyone can build one or pick up where this leaves off.

Where it stands

Parts

Ordered 30 October 2026

A Bluetooth board with a built-in motion sensor, a haptic driver, two linear vibration motors, a 100 mAh battery, magnets, ribbon and a soldering iron. About $75 of parts and $80 of tools.

Everything is off the shelf from Digi-Key and Amazon. The shopping list with links and prices is on the build sheet.

Shopping list

Shells

Designed, not yet printed

Two cups and two lids, 36 mm across, with the magnets set into the floors. A 15 mm ribbon joins them and carries four wires from the outer disc to the motor in the inner one.

The print files are below. Two smaller dummy discs are there to check the fit on a shirt before the real ones are printed.

Print files

Firmware

Compiles, not yet run on the board

The board streams its motion sensor to the phone 25 times a second and drives the motor from the strength the phone sends 50 times a second. If the phone goes quiet for a second, the motor stops.

About 400 lines of Arduino. The motor driver calibrates itself once to the motor and remembers the result.

How it works

Phone app

iPhone, builds and passes its tests

Reads breathing from the motion sensor, counts breaths per minute, tells inhale from exhale, and pauses when you are walking. Three paced patterns, or a mode that follows your own breath.

Until the clip exists, the app does the same thing with the phone resting on your chest, so the breathing detector was tested first against made-up chest movement at 5, 12 and 20 breaths a minute.

Shells to print

Binary STL, millimetres. Print in resin or a fine PLA with the open side up. The lid is printed twice.

  • outer_cup_v0.stl, 36 × 12 mm, holds the board and battery
  • inner_cup_v0.stl, 36 × 8 mm, holds the motor
  • lid_v0.stl, 36 × 1.2 mm, print two
  • dummy_outer_32x9.stl and dummy_inner_32x6.stl, solid discs at the size the next version aims for, to check how it sits on a shirt

What comes next

In the next few weeks

  • Print the shells and check the magnets hold through a t-shirt and a sweater.
  • Solder the ten wires, run the motor calibration, and feel the first swell.
  • Wear it at the neckline, the cuff and the hem, and see where the breathing signal is cleanest.
  • Put the app's source and the firmware on GitHub and link them here.

If it works

  • A single custom circuit board so the discs shrink from 36 mm to 32 mm.
  • Heart rate and heart rate variability from a watch, next to the breathing.
  • A quieter, lower-frequency motor, which is what makes a vibration feel calm rather than like a phone buzzing.
